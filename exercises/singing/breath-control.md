# Breath Control

## Goal

Sustain notes longer, control phrase length, and maintain consistent volume and tone throughout a sung phrase. Good breath control means you never run out of air mid-phrase or compensate by tensing.

## Duration

15 minutes

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Sustained Tone (The Long Note)
1. Take a full diaphragmatic breath (see `projection/diaphragm-breathing.md`).
2. Sing a comfortable mid-range note on "ah" for as long as you can while maintaining:
   - Steady pitch (use a tuner)
   - Even volume (no swell or fade)
   - Relaxed throat
3. Time yourself. Record your baseline duration.
4. Rest 30 seconds. Repeat 3 times.
5. Goal: 15+ seconds of clean, sustained tone.

### Part 2 — Phrase Pacing
1. Take a single breath.
2. Sing a 4-bar phrase of a song you know (or "Happy Birthday" as a stand-in).
3. You should arrive at the end of the phrase with breath to spare. If you run out, you inhaled too little or released air too fast.
4. Work on *managing* the air — don't dump it all in the first bar.
5. Repeat 5 times, focusing on holding back air until needed.

### Part 3 — Staccato-to-Legato
1. Sing a scale in staccato (short, detached notes) — this engages the diaphragm sharply.
2. Immediately sing the same scale in legato (connected, smooth notes) — this requires steady release.
3. Alternating between them builds diaphragm control and awareness.
4. Repeat 3 times per key.

### Part 4 — Silent Breath Drill
1. Without singing, practice the breath pattern of a song you know.
2. Inhale at the spots where a singer would breathe, exhale through the phrase silently.
3. Mark the breathing spots that feel natural vs. forced — these are your breath management cues for when you add voice.

## Common Mistakes

- **Taking too big a breath** — oversized breaths cause tension and uneven release. Take enough, not everything you can hold.
- **Tensing the throat to make air last** — this squeezes the tone. The diaphragm controls the pace of release, not the throat.
- **Letting volume drop at phrase ends** — support all the way through the last note. A phrase ends on pitch and volume, not a fade.
