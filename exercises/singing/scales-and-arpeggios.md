# Scales and Arpeggios

## Goal

Build vocal range, warm up the voice systematically, and develop evenness of tone across registers. Scales are not a performance — they are a diagnostic and a training tool.

## Duration

10–15 minutes daily (never skip this warm-up before other singing work)

## Tools Needed

- Piano, keyboard app, or a scale track (search "vocal warm up scales piano" on YouTube)
- Chromatic tuner (optional but useful)

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Five-Note Scale (1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1)
1. Start on a comfortable low note (not your lowest).
2. Sing up 5 notes on "mah" or "may", then back down.
3. Move up one half-step and repeat.
4. Continue until you reach the top of your comfortable range.
5. Then do the same moving downward from your start point.
6. Total: about 5 minutes.

**Focus:** Even tone from note to note. Don't let any single note sound different in quality.

### Part 2 — Octave Scale (1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1)
1. Start slightly higher than your warm-up starting note.
2. Sing the full major scale up and back down on "ah" or "oh".
3. Move up by half-steps until you find your upper passaggio (where the voice wants to flip or strain).
4. At the passaggio, don't push — sing through it lightly. This is where the voice develops over time.
5. Total: 5 minutes.

### Part 3 — Arpeggios (1-3-5-8-5-3-1)
1. Sing the root, major third, fifth, and octave in ascending then descending order.
2. Use "ah" vowel throughout.
3. Work through 8 keys across your range.
4. This develops interval accuracy and agility simultaneously.

## Common Mistakes

- **Pushing through strain** — if a note hurts, stop. Go to the note just below and call that your ceiling for today.
- **Changing vowel shape across the range** — the mouth position should stay consistent. Don't let "ah" turn into something else as pitch rises.
- **Racing through it** — slow enough to hear each note clearly. Scales are precision work.
- **Skipping the warm-up** — singing on a cold voice risks damage and yields poor results.
