# Pitch Matching

## Goal

Train your ear to hear a pitch accurately and your voice to reproduce it precisely. This is the most fundamental singing skill — everything else depends on it.

## Duration

15 minutes

## Tools Needed

- A piano, keyboard app, or a free tuner app (e.g., GarageBand, Vocal Pitch Monitor)
- A chromatic tuner to verify your pitch (Pano Tuner is free and accurate)

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Single Note Matching
1. Play a note in your comfortable mid-range (for most voices: around C3–C4).
2. Listen for 3 seconds without singing.
3. Hum the note. Hold it for 5 seconds.
4. Open the tuner — are you in tune? How many cents off?
5. Adjust and try again. Repeat until you land within 10 cents consistently.
6. Move up one half-step and repeat.
7. Work through 8–10 notes per session.

### Part 2 — Interval Training
1. Play a note. Sing it.
2. Play the note a major third up (4 half-steps). Sing it without playing it first — predict it.
3. Check with the tuner.
4. Common intervals to drill: unison, major 2nd, major 3rd, perfect 4th, perfect 5th, octave.
5. Spend 2 minutes per interval.

### Part 3 — Drone Matching
1. Play a sustained note (or use a drone app).
2. Sing any melody you know over the drone, coming back to the root note at phrase ends.
3. Listen for when you are in tune vs. slightly off. Train yourself to hear the difference — it's a beating or wavering sound when you're out of tune.

## Common Mistakes

- **Singing flat on the way up** — when intervals rise, most singers undershoot. Consciously overshoot first to recalibrate.
- **Not using a tuner** — your ear is unreliable at the start. Objective feedback is essential.
- **Tensing up** — pitch accuracy gets worse under tension. Relax jaw, neck, and shoulders before each note.
