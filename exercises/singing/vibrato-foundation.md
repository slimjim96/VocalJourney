# Vibrato Foundation

## Goal

Develop a natural, even vibrato — the gentle pitch oscillation that adds warmth and expression to sustained notes. Vibrato should not be forced; it emerges from relaxed, supported tone.

## Duration

10 minutes (introduce only after pitch and breath control are stable — typically Week 5+)

## Prerequisites

Comfortable with:
- Diaphragmatic breathing
- Sustaining a note for 10+ seconds cleanly
- Consistent pitch accuracy on scales

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Release, Don't Force
1. Sing a sustained note on "ah" at a comfortable mid-range pitch.
2. Hold it for 5 seconds with a completely straight (non-vibrato) tone.
3. Gradually relax your jaw, tongue, and throat without changing the note.
4. For many singers, a light natural oscillation will appear on its own when tension releases.
5. Don't try to create the oscillation — just remove the obstacles to it.

### Part 2 — Diaphragm Pulsing
1. Sing a sustained note and deliberately pulse your diaphragm rhythmically (similar to a slow "huh-huh-huh" motion, but without stopping the tone).
2. Aim for about 5–6 pulses per second.
3. This is not real vibrato — it's a training tool to feel the diaphragm's role in oscillation.
4. Practice 2 minutes. Then sing without the deliberate pulse and notice if any natural oscillation remains.

### Part 3 — Jaw Oscillation Technique
1. Sing a note. While sustaining it, gently oscillate your jaw up and down at a regular pace.
2. This creates an audible pitch oscillation.
3. Gradually reduce jaw movement over 10 seconds while trying to maintain the oscillation through breath support alone.
4. The goal: the vibrato continues even when the jaw is still.

### Part 4 — Vibrato on a Phrase
1. Sing a short 2-bar phrase.
2. Apply a gentle vibrato only on the final held note of each phrase.
3. Don't apply it everywhere — vibrato on every note sounds unnatural.
4. Focus on starting the note straight, then letting the vibrato settle in after the first beat.

## Common Mistakes

- **Forcing it** — vibrato that's manually wobbled sounds uncontrolled and unstable. It should feel effortless.
- **Too fast or too slow** — natural vibrato oscillates at 5–7 cycles per second. Slower is a wobble, faster is a tremor.
- **Introducing vibrato too early** — build a clean, straight tone first. Vibrato on top of poor technique makes both worse.
- **Vibrato covering pitch problems** — you should be able to remove the vibrato and have a clean, in-tune straight tone underneath.
