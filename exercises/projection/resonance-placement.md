# Resonance Placement

## Goal

Move vocal resonance from the throat into the chest and forward facial mask (cheekbones, sinuses, lips). Forward placement projects without strain and gives the voice richness and carry.

## Duration

10 minutes

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Find the Mask (Humming)
1. Hum a comfortable mid-pitch note ("mmmmm").
2. Place two fingers on your lips — you should feel vibration. If you don't, the sound is sitting too far back.
3. Slowly glide pitch up and down while keeping fingers on your lips. Maintain the buzz.
4. Do this for 2 minutes.

### Part 2 — Chest Resonance (Low Register)
1. Speak or hum a low note and place your palm flat on your chest.
2. You should feel vibration in your sternum. This is chest resonance — it adds depth and authority.
3. Say "one, two, three, four, five" slowly at a low pitch while feeling for chest buzz.
4. Repeat 5 times.

### Part 3 — "NG" Placement Exercise
1. Say "NGGGG" as in "sing" — hold it for 3 seconds. This forces forward resonance.
2. Transition directly from "NG" into a vowel: "NGGGG-ah", "NGGGG-oh", "NGGGG-ee".
3. The vowel should carry the same forward buzz as the NG.
4. Repeat with each vowel 5 times each.

### Part 4 — Project to the Back of the Room
1. Stand at one end of a room. Imagine speaking to someone at the far end.
2. Count from 1 to 10 without raising your volume — instead, increase *forward placement* and support.
3. Record yourself and listen back. Does the voice sound full and clear, or thin and effortful?

## Common Mistakes

- **Pushing from the throat** — causes strain and a tight, pinched sound. If your throat feels tired, you're working the wrong muscle. Relax the throat, engage the breath.
- **Confusing loud with projected** — projection is about *direction* and *resonance*, not volume. A well-placed quiet voice carries further than a strained loud one.
- **Losing the buzz on vowels** — most people place consonants forward and then pull back on vowels. Keep the buzz through the whole phrase.
