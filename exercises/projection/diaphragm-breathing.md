# Diaphragm Breathing

## Goal

Train the diaphragm to drive breath rather than the chest. Diaphragmatic breathing gives you a sustained, controlled airflow — the foundation of projection and singing.

## Duration

10 minutes per session (5 min lying down, 5 min standing)

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Feel the Diaphragm (Lying Down)
1. Lie flat on your back, knees bent, one hand on your chest, one on your belly.
2. Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 counts. Your belly should rise, your chest should barely move.
3. Hold for 2 counts.
4. Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 counts. Feel your belly fall.
5. Repeat 10 times.

### Part 2 — Standing Breath
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Relax your shoulders.
2. Place one hand on your belly just below your ribcage.
3. Inhale for 4 counts, belly expands outward.
4. Exhale on a sustained "sss" sound for 8–10 counts. Keep the sound even — no wavering.
5. Repeat 8 times.

### Part 3 — Pulsed Breath (Bellows)
1. Exhale sharply through your mouth in short bursts: huh-huh-huh (8 pulses).
2. You should feel your belly pump inward with each pulse.
3. Rest, then repeat 5 times.
4. This trains diaphragmatic engagement and quick breath recovery.

## Common Mistakes

- **Shoulders rising on inhale** — means you're chest-breathing. Consciously drop your shoulders before each breath.
- **Running out of air too fast** — means you're pushing from the throat, not the diaphragm. Let the belly control the pace.
- **Rushing the exhale** — slow it down. Sustained, controlled exhale = sustained, controlled voice.
