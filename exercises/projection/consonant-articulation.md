# Consonant Articulation

## Goal

Develop crisp, precise consonants so your voice is understood clearly at distance and at normal volume. Muddy consonants kill projection — people hear sound but not words.

## Duration

10–15 minutes

## Instructions

### Part 1 — Lip Trills (Warm Up)
1. Blow air through loose, closed lips to create a trill (like a motorboat sound).
2. Hold for 5 seconds. Repeat 5 times.
3. This loosens the lips and prepares them for precise articulation work.

### Part 2 — Tongue Twisters (Slow Then Fast)
Work each one slowly first — exaggerating every consonant — then bring up the speed.

- **P/B drill:** "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers."
- **T/D drill:** "Toy boat, toy boat, toy boat."
- **K/G drill:** "Can you can a can as a canner can can a can?"
- **S/Sh drill:** "She sells seashells by the seashore."
- **L/R drill:** "Red leather, yellow leather, red leather, yellow leather."

Protocol for each:
1. Say it slowly, over-articulating every sound (20 seconds).
2. Say it at natural speed with crisp consonants (10 seconds).
3. Say it fast without losing clarity (10 seconds).

### Part 3 — Passage Reading (Out Loud)
1. Pick any paragraph from a book.
2. Read it aloud at 70% of your normal pace, exaggerating consonants.
3. Increase pace gradually until you're at normal speed, maintaining crispness.
4. Record and listen back. Focus on whether every consonant is distinct.

### Part 4 — Distance Test
1. Stand 10–15 feet from a wall or partner (or record yourself at a distance).
2. Speak a sentence clearly without shouting.
3. If it sounds muddy or unclear, slow down and sharpen the consonants.

## Common Mistakes

- **Dropping final consonants** — "goin'" instead of "going", "an'" instead of "and". Consciously close each word.
- **Lazy lips** — the biggest culprit for mumbling. Exaggerate lip movement in practice even if it feels silly.
- **Speeding through drills** — speed without clarity is worthless. Lock in the form before increasing pace.
